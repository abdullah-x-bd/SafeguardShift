# SafeguardShift V2 frozen hypotheses

H1 Directional adaptation. Removing a task-relevant safeguard will reduce attempted high-impact action and increase reversible or information-gathering action relative to the matched full condition.

H2 Selectivity. Behavioral change after relevant safeguard ablation will exceed behavioral change after irrelevant safeguard ablation.

H3 Safeguard heterogeneity. Adaptation effects will differ across verification, expert review, monitoring, reversibility, authority, and execution capacity; V2 estimates each safeguard on an exactly balanced task set.

H4 Substitution use. When a substitute explicitly restores the missing safeguard and unlocks a higher-utility safe action, agents will sometimes activate and use it. The estimand is the rate and interval, not a post-hoc success threshold.

H5 Substitution utility gain. Utility in the substitute condition will exceed utility in ordinary relevant-ablation condition to the extent agents exploit the replacement safeguard.

H6 Repeated-run reliability. Agent behavior will show non-trivial within-cell variability; terminal pass^3 and action-set consistency are pre-specified reliability outcomes.

H7 Tool reliability separation. Tool-schema/terminal-contract failures are reported separately from unsafe policy behavior and are not silently imputed as safe or unsafe decisions.

H8 Ranking stability. Model ordering may change across safeguard conditions and safeguard types; rankings are descriptive and secondary.
